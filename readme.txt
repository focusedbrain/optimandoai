## 🧩 Covered Framework Components

The Canon defines, among other components:

### Optirando™

**Make Everything Smart™**

The browser-based orchestration framework for contextual, governed and
template-driven automation across agents, services, devices, software
systems and physical environments.

### WR Code®

**Workflow Ready Code**

The transport-agnostic identification, context-selection and workflow
activation layer of the Optirando™ ecosystem.

WR Code® may be transported through channels such as QR codes, NFC,
display codes or other supported identification mechanisms. The
transport mechanism itself does not replace the required identity,
governance or handshake procedures defined by the Canon.

### BEAP™

**Bidirectional Email Automation Protocol**

The canonical protocol for secure, structured and bidirectional
automation workflows transmitted through email, messaging and related
communication environments.

### PoAC™

PoAC™ is the canonical admission and governance discipline for the
reviewed, authorized, attributable and revocable admission of workflows,
sources, adapters, manifests, capabilities, policies and other artifacts
into an authoritative WR™ environment.

The complete normative meaning and requirements of PoAC™ are defined
exclusively by Paper v202 and its incorporated Annexes.

### PoAE™

**Proof of Authoritative Execution**

The canonical cryptographic verification and evidence layer for
execution that is attributable, identity-bound, intent-bound,
policy-bound, scope-bound and authorized according to the governance
rules of the Canon.

The complete normative meaning and requirements of PoAE™ are defined
exclusively by Paper v202 and its incorporated Annexes.

## 📝 Source Integrity and OpenTimestamps

This repository uses OpenTimestamps (`.ots`) proofs to provide
independently verifiable evidence of publication chronology and file
integrity through the Bitcoin blockchain.

### Verification

Core files may have corresponding `.ots` proof files, including:

- the Canonical Paper;
- incorporated Annexes;
- license files;
- governance documents;
- and other significant records.

### Audit Trail

The timestamp records help demonstrate:

- that a specific file existed by a verifiable point in time;
- that the timestamped file has not been altered;
- the historical development of the Canon;
- and the provenance of important publications.

An OpenTimestamps proof does not itself determine copyright,
inventorship, trademark ownership, validity or legal enforceability.
It provides cryptographic evidence relating to the existence and
integrity of the timestamped file.
